Turkey-Syria JETCO protocol signed: New era in economic partnership

Trade Minister Ömer Bolat said that they have made an offer to this country regarding the establishment of a new generation comprehensive economic partnership agreement between Turkey and Syria.

At the Turkey-Syria Joint Economic and Trade Committee (JETCO) Founding Protocol Signing Ceremony held in Ankara on August 5, 2025, Trade Minister Bolat and Syrian Economy and Industry Minister Nidal Shaar came together. Bolat stated that 'a positive atmosphere has emerged' in economic relations with the newly established government in Syria, emphasizing that the trade volume, which was $2.6 billion in 2024, reached $1.9 billion in the first 7 months of 2025. Bolat explained that they have proposed a comprehensive economic partnership agreement targeting the integration of the two countries' economies instead of the old free trade agreement, and that bilateral cooperation will accelerate with the JETCO mechanism. It was announced that the international road transport agreement signed in June 2025 will accelerate trade, the modernization of customs gates continues, and capacity will be increased at 7 active border gates. In the talks, a memorandum of understanding on administrative and governance cooperation was also signed to strengthen Syria's institutional capacity in trade, customs and economy. It was also stated that the Joint Business Council between Turkey and Syria will be re-established and Turkish banks are preparing to operate in Syria. Syrian Minister Shaar, on the other hand, expressed that they see Turkey as a 'strategic partner' and want the economies to integrate through joint production and mutual investments. The two ministers laid the foundation for a new era in economic relations by signing a memorandum of understanding on institutional capacity building in addition to the protocol.

At least half of Syrians will return

After the fall of the Baath regime, Syrians began to return to their country. Syrian Businessmen Association President Halit Savafoğlu said, "We think that at least half of the Syrians living in Turkey will return. Those who see that their country has improved will go back. However, we cannot completely break our ties. We have a debt of gratitude to this country. We ate bread here. We are grateful to Turkey."

Syrian economy growing faster than expected, sanctions easing

Central Bank of Syria Governor Abdulkadir Husriye announced that with the return of refugees and the end of the 14-year civil war, the economy grew much faster in 2025 than the 1% growth rate predicted by the World Bank.
