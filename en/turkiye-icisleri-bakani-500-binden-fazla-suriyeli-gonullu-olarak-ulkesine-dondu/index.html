Turkish Interior Minister: More than 500,000 Syrians voluntarily returned to their country

ANKARA (SANA) – Turkish Interior Minister Ali Yerlikaya announced that the number of Syrians who have voluntarily returned to their country since the collapse of the overthrown regime on December 8, 2024 has exceeded 500,000. Yerlikaya emphasized that Turkey continues to stand by Syrians in their voluntary return process.

Minister Yerlikaya announced in his post on the 'En Sosyal' platform as of December 2025 that a total of 509,387 Syrians have voluntarily returned to their country since December 8, 2024. Thus, the total number of Syrians who have left Turkey since 2016 has reached 1 million 249 thousand. Yerlikaya expressed that Turkey, as in the past, continues to provide the necessary support for the honorable and safe return of their Syrian brothers. The return of Syrians to their country is considered an important development that will contribute to the reconstruction and economic recovery process on the Syrian side while affecting the labor market in Turkey. The return of many Syrian business people to their country with the experience they gained from the businesses they established in Turkey could accelerate Syria's economic revival. Experts state that the safe return of Syrian refugees will have positive reflections both on the Turkish economy and on Syria's reconstruction.

Business world representatives expect the new era in Syria to create new opportunities for Turkey.

At least half of Syrians will return

After the fall of the Baath regime, Syrians began to return to their country. Syrian Businessmen Association President Halit Savafoğlu said, "We think that at least half of the Syrians living in Turkey will return. Those who see that their country has improved will go back. However, we cannot completely break our ties. We have a debt of gratitude to this country. We ate bread here. We are grateful to Turkey."

Central Bank of Syria Governor Abdulkadir Husriye announced that with the return of refugees and the end of the 14-year civil war, the economy grew much faster in 2025 than the 1% growth rate predicted by the World Bank.
