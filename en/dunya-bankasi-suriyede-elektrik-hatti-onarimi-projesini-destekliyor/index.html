World Bank supports electricity line repair project in Syria

DAMASCUS (SANA) – Syrian Finance Minister Mohammed Yasser Berniyye announced that the final document regarding the electricity transmission line repair project with the World Bank has been signed. This project is the World Bank's first project in Syria after approximately forty years.

In December 2025, the World Bank signed the final agreement for a $146 million grant project to repair electricity transmission lines damaged in conflicts in Syria. Finance Minister Berniyye stated that the project will contribute to the efforts of the Syrian state – particularly the Ministry of Energy – to improve the provision of electricity needed to support the economy. He also announced that there are other projects in preparation with the World Bank in different sectors such as energy, transportation, and water, which will also be financed through grants and will be announced to the public in 2026. This agreement was recorded as the World Bank's first investment project in Syria since the 1980s and shows that international financial institutions are beginning to re-engage in Syria's reconstruction process. The World Bank Board of Directors approved this project in June 2025, and during the project's preparation process, teams from the Syrian Ministries of Energy, Foreign Affairs, and Finance worked together with World Bank teams. If the project is successfully implemented, a significant improvement in electricity supply across Syria is expected; this will accelerate the recovery of critical sectors such as industry, health, and education. The Syrian government hopes that with the implementation of the project, the electricity needed by the national economy will be provided more stably and doors will open to other international support programs.
