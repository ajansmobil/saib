Digital transformation agreement between Syrian Engineers Union and Saudi Ejad Tech

DAMASCUS (SANA) – The Syrian Engineers Union signed a cooperation agreement with Ejad Tech company, a subsidiary of Saudi Arabia-based GO Telecom group, at the union headquarters in Damascus.

The signed memorandum of understanding creates a comprehensive digital cooperation framework between the two institutions and covers especially the development of digital systems in the engineering field, support for digital transformation projects and the provision of advanced technical consulting services. The purpose of this agreement was stated as increasing efficiency in engineering work and raising service quality. Engineers Union President Malik Haj Ali emphasized that the agreement will strengthen technical and professional cooperation between Syrian and Saudi engineers and will enable Syrian engineers to participate more effectively in digital transformation projects. Ejad Tech CEO Mohammed Al-Bukmi expressed that this initiative is part of Saudi Arabia's support approach to Syrian institutions, especially the Engineers Union. Al-Bukmi stated that they aim to offer employment opportunities for qualified Syrian engineers in Saudi Arabia and in the company's projects in Syria. The agreement also envisages cooperation in areas such as technical experience sharing, improvement of logistics infrastructure and integration of digital platforms. This step is seen as part of Syria's efforts to expand cooperation in engineering and technology at the international level and will contribute to the integration of Syrian engineers' knowledge into regional projects.

Business world representatives expect the new era in Syria to create new opportunities for Turkey.

At least half of Syrians will return

After the fall of the Baath regime, Syrians began to return to their country. Syrian Businessmen Association President Halit Savafoğlu said, "We think that at least half of the Syrians living in Turkey will return. Those who see that their country has improved will go back. However, we cannot completely break our ties. We have a debt of gratitude to this country. We ate bread here. We are grateful to Turkey."

Syrian economy growing faster than expected, sanctions easing

Central Bank of Syria Governor Abdulkadir Husriye announced that with the return of refugees and the end of the 14-year civil war, the economy grew much faster in 2025 than the 1% growth rate predicted by the World Bank.
