Syria International Oil, Gas and Energy Fair (SOG) begins in Damascus

The Syria International Oil, Gas and Energy Fair (SOG), attended by 90 local, Arab and international companies operating in Syria's oil, gas and energy sector, opened at the Damascus Fairground.

The fair held in December 2025 is considered an indicator of the government's determination to strengthen the oil and energy sector. The fair offers opportunities for cooperation and experience sharing between investors and technical experts. In his opening speech, Deputy Energy Minister Geyyas Diyab emphasized the importance of this event that brings together local, Arab and foreign companies despite all difficulties, and said that the energy sector is one of the cornerstones of reconstruction and development. Diyab stated that qualified steps have been taken recently to facilitate investment in the oil sector and attract foreign companies, legislation has been updated, and the establishment of the Syrian Petroleum Company (SPC) has contributed to the ministry's more effective and transparent management of oil resources. Italy participated officially in the fair for the first time, and Italy's Ambassador to Damascus Stefano Ravagnan announced that seven Italian companies were present at the fair; some were already in the market before 2011, while others were looking for new investment opportunities. Ravagnan emphasized the revitalization of energy cooperation and the utilization of local resources. Within the scope of the fair, the latest technologies in oil-gas exploration, drilling, refining, transportation, storage, logistics and renewable energy were exhibited. The SOG Fair remained open to visitors for a week and increased international interest in the Syrian energy sector by accelerating information exchange in the sector.

Damascus Fair will open new cooperation doors in Turkey-Syria trade

Turkey-Syria Business Forum held in Aleppo

The Turkey-Syria Business Forum related to the construction and building materials sector was held in Aleppo, northern Syria.

Business world focuses on economic impacts of developments in Syria

Business world representatives expect the new era in Syria to create new opportunities for Turkey.

At least half of Syrians will return

After the fall of the Baath regime, Syrians began to return to their country. Syrian Businessmen Association President Halit Savafoğlu said, "We think that at least half of the Syrians living in Turkey will return. Those who see that their country has improved will go back. However, we cannot completely break our ties. We have a debt of gratitude to this country. We ate bread here. We are grateful to Turkey."

Syrian economy growing faster than expected, sanctions easing

Central Bank of Syria Governor Abdulkadir Husriye announced that with the return of refugees and the end of the 14-year civil war, the economy grew much faster in 2025 than the 1% growth rate predicted by the World Bank.
